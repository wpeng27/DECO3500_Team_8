Our plan of work has four stages between now and the prototype submission. First, research and validation — we'll run four to six interviews with students who've experienced the anxiety of forming groups with strangers, plus a couple of interviews with domain experts like peer mentors or tutors who observe this pattern across many students. We'll use story-eliciting questions to get at specific moments and underlying values, rather than generic opinions.

Second, we'll converge on a concept. Right now we have four candidate features — course-bound spaces, progressive anonymity, a time-limited window, and an online-to-offline handoff card. Interview data will help us decide which of these matter most, and resolve an open question we still have: how much anonymity do students actually want, versus how much starts to feel isolating rather than safe?

Third is prototyping. Because our concept depends on multi-user, asynchronous interaction — one student posting anonymously, another discovering and matching with them over time — a simple click-through Figma prototype won't capture that experience. We're planning to simulate multiple user perspectives so we can actually test the matching flow, not just individual screens.

Finally, we'll test with three to five target students, focusing specifically on whether the anonymity mechanism feels safe rather than confusing, and whether the time-limited window feels motivating rather than another source of pressure — then iterate based on what we learn.
