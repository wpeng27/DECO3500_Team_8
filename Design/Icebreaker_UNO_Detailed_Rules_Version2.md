# Icebreaker — Detailed Staged Interaction Rules

These rules are a design proposal for the Icebreaker prototype and are not rules prescribed by Olsson et al.

The screen manages the progression of social interaction, while physical UNO remains the main shared activity.

## 1. Base Game Rules

- Players use a normal physical UNO deck.

- The prototype is designed primarily for four players, although three to five players can still be supported.

- Each player starts with seven cards.

- Normal UNO turn order and standard card-play rules apply unless the screen temporarily introduces a social challenge.

- The screen does not need to electronically track every physical card.

- Normal UNO should continue between social challenges.

- A social challenge should usually last between 30 and 90 seconds or one full round of turns.

- Players should never be required to reveal personal information or discuss whether they want to form an assignment group with another player.

Suggested rhythm: Normal UNO → Stage 1 → Normal UNO → Stage 2 → Normal UNO → Stage 3 → Normal UNO → Stage 4 → Normal UNO → Stage 5.

## 2. Stage 1 — Colour Call

Purpose: create the lowest-pressure form of shared participation.

Everyone participates together, but nobody needs to directly address another player.

### Screen Prompt

> COLOUR CALL

> For the next four turns, everyone says the active colour together.

> The screen shows how many turns remain.

### Detailed Rules

- Whenever a player places a card, everyone says the active colour aloud together.

- If the colour does not change, everyone still repeats the current colour.

- If a Wild or Wild Draw Four is played, the player first chooses the new colour.

- After the player announces the chosen colour, everyone repeats it together.

- A turn counts toward the four-turn total even when the active colour stays the same.

- There is no individual winner for this challenge.

- The challenge is complete after four turns.

- The screen then displays: Warm-up complete — back to normal UNO.

## 3. Stage 2 — Support Move

Purpose: move players from shared participation toward noticing and helping another person.

### Screen Prompt

> SUPPORT MOVE

> Before your turn, ask one UNO-related question or offer one useful suggestion to another player.

### Detailed Rules

- The screen selects one player at a time to complete a Support Move.

- The selected player must briefly interact with one other player before taking their normal turn.

- The interaction must stay related to the game.

- Example questions include: How many cards do you have?

- Another example is: What colour would help you?

- Another example is: Would red help you next turn?

- Players may also offer a suggestion instead of asking a question.

- No player is required to reveal their hand.

- No player is required to follow the advice they receive.

- The goal is to create a short interaction, not to optimise the game.

- Each player completes one Support Move.

- For four players, this creates four support interactions in total.

- The screen tracks completion using simple check marks for each player.

- Once everyone has completed one Support Move, the screen returns the table to normal UNO.

- The screen should choose whose turn it is to complete the interaction rather than asking for volunteers.

- This avoids placing the burden of social initiation on the players themselves.

## 4. Stage 3 — Partner Decision

Purpose: create a short and structured one-to-one interaction.

### Screen Prompt

> PARTNER DECISION

> The screen temporarily pairs two players.

> The pair receives a 20-second task related to the next UNO move.

### Detailed Rules

- One player becomes the Active Player and the other becomes the Partner.

- The Active Player describes their playable options to the Partner.

- If they are comfortable doing so, the Active Player may show only the playable options rather than their whole hand.

- The pair discusses which card the Active Player should play.

- The Partner may advise, but cannot physically play the card.

- The Active Player makes the final move.

- The pair has 20 seconds to reach a decision.

- If they do not agree before the timer ends, the Active Player chooses the move.

- The screen should not display the word Failed when time runs out.

- Instead, the screen should display: Time's up — Active Player chooses.

- For four players, two Partner Decision rounds are enough to involve everyone.

- The first pair can be Players 1 and 3, and the second pair can be Players 2 and 4.

- The partnership lasts for only one short task.

- The screen then clearly states that the temporary partnership is complete.

- This avoids implying that the system is matching students into assignment groups.

### Alternative Partner Task — Shared Colour

- The two temporary partners must agree on one target colour.

- They then try to make that colour active within their next two combined turns.

- The task ends after the two combined turns even if the target colour is not achieved.

## 5. Stage 4 — Team Round

Purpose: temporarily replace individual competition with a whole-table shared objective.

### Primary Shared Objective

> TEAM ROUND

> Individual winning is paused.

> Get every player to five cards or fewer before the 90-second timer ends.

### Detailed Rules

- During the Team Round, nobody can win individually.

- If a player reaches zero cards, they remain part of the challenge until the team objective is complete.

- A player with zero cards can continue helping the group through discussion and planning.

- Normal UNO card mechanics still apply.

- Players still play matching colours, numbers and action cards.

- Reverse, Skip, Draw Two and Wild cards continue to behave normally.

- Players are encouraged to openly discuss strategy during this round.

- For example, players may suggest changing the colour to help another player.

- They may also decide to avoid a move that would make another player draw extra cards.

- The screen displays each player's current hand count.

- For the prototype, the hand counts can be entered manually rather than automatically detected.

- The table succeeds when every player has five cards or fewer before time expires.

- If the timer ends before the goal is reached, the screen should not display Failed.

- Instead, it can display how many players reached the target.

- For example, the screen can display: Team Round complete — 3 of 4 players reached the target.

### Alternative Shared Objectives

- No One Left Behind: Every player must successfully play one card before anyone is allowed to play a second card.

- Rescue the Table: The player with the largest hand must lose at least three cards before the timer ends.

- Colour Sequence: The table must collectively create the sequence Red → Yellow → Green → Blue.

- Balanced Hands: Get every player's hand within two cards of each other.

For the first prototype, the five-cards-or-fewer objective is the simplest to explain and test.

## 6. Stage 5 — Interdependence Round

Purpose: give players different responsibilities so that they need one another to complete the challenge.

### Screen Prompt

> INTERDEPENDENCE ROUND

> Everyone has a temporary role.

> Complete one full table rotation before the timer ends.

### Player 1 — Guide

- The Guide may recommend moves to other players.

- During the Guide's own turn, another player must advise them.

- The Guide cannot control another player's final decision.

### Player 2 — Rule Keeper

- The Rule Keeper is responsible for explaining the temporary challenge if anyone becomes confused.

- Other players are encouraged to ask the Rule Keeper for clarification instead of relying only on the screen.

### Player 3 — Supporter

- The Supporter may give one other player a second opinion before a move.

- The screen can show Support Available: 1 and change it to Support Used after the ability is used.

- No NFC or wearable technology is required; the player or facilitator can simply tap the screen.

### Player 4 — Coordinator

- Before each player moves, the Coordinator checks that everyone understands the current shared objective.

- The Coordinator can remind the group what still needs to be completed.

- The Coordinator does not control other players' moves.

### Shared Objective for Stage 5

> EVERYONE PLAYS

> Every player must successfully complete one turn.

> Nobody may be skipped out of the full rotation.

> The timer is two minutes.

- If a Skip card would prevent another player from completing their required turn, the group must decide whether using it is appropriate.

- The challenge therefore connects UNO mechanics, temporary roles, social coordination and a shared objective.

### Optional Advanced Stage 5 — Role Puzzle

- The screen gives the table one shared sequence objective.

- Example objective: play exactly four cards consisting of one red card, one action card, one number below five and one Wild card.

- Every player must contribute to completing the sequence.

- The Guide suggests strategy.

- The Rule Keeper remembers the requirements.

- The Coordinator tracks which requirement the table is currently trying to complete.

- The Supporter may allow the team to reconsider one decision.

- This version should only be tested after the simpler role round works reliably.

## 7. How the Screen Manages Progression

The screen should not judge whether players are social enough.

It only tracks which types of interaction have already occurred.

A simple progress display can show: Shared response complete, Support interaction complete, Partner interaction complete, Group coordination complete, Interdependence not yet complete.

After one type of interaction is complete, the screen can introduce the next level later in the game.

The screen therefore remembers the interaction history of the session.

This gives the technology a stronger purpose than randomly selecting a paper rule from a hat.

## 8. Suggested 15–20 Minute Prototype Flow

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3 min | Normal UNO |
| 3–4 min | Stage 1 — Colour Call |
| 4–6 min | Normal UNO |
| 6–8 min | Stage 2 — Support Move |
| 8–10 min | Normal UNO |
| 10–12 min | Stage 3 — Partner Decision |
| 12–14 min | Normal UNO |
| 14–16 min | Stage 4 — Team Round |
| 16–19 min | Stage 5 — Interdependence Round |
| 19–20 min | Finish and short reflection |

The repeated return to normal UNO is important.

The experience should feel like UNO with occasional social interventions.

It should not feel like a social exercise disguised as a card game.

This distinction should be evaluated during user testing because it directly affects whether the experience feels low pressure.
