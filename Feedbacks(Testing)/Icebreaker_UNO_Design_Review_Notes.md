# Icebreaker UNO Design Review Notes

## Feedback on Our UNO Icebreaker

### 1. Suggestions for Our UNO Icebreaker Game

Forcing players to answer a question during every turn would slow down the pace of UNO.

Players may feel that the questions interrupt the game and may eventually stop playing and only chat.

This would weaken UNO’s original gameplay. Therefore, the question mechanism should be integrated with the action cards.

### 2. Connecting Questions with Action Cards

Skip card: The skipped player can ask the person who played the card a question as compensation or a form of counterattack.

Cost of playing an action card: If players want to use an action card against someone, they must accept the possibility of answering that person’s question.

When playing a card, players need to consider what advantage the card provides and whether they are willing to answer the other player’s question.

Players who enjoy chatting may use action cards more actively, while players who are less comfortable answering questions may play more carefully.

This allows social interaction to become a natural part of the game instead of forcing everyone to talk on every turn.

### 3. Ideas for Further Development

Different question or interaction rules could be designed for different UNO action cards.

The winner of each round could receive a special privilege, such as choosing the question theme for the next round or selecting a category like “university life,” “hobbies,” or “shared interests.”

This would give the winner an extra reward and make the questions different in each round.

### 4. Problems Identified During Current Testing

When three people tested the game, playing cards while asking questions easily turned into a normal conversation.

Once the conversation started, players gradually lost interest in continuing the UNO game.

Therefore, further testing is needed to determine whether the question mechanism interrupts the pace of the game.

We also need to observe whether players with different personalities use action cards less often because they do not want to answer questions.

## Notes on Another Group's Design

### 5. Another Group’s Post-Game Reflection Design

Another group designed a reflection tool that players use after finishing a multiplayer game.

It mainly addresses situations in which players feel uncomfortable directly criticising their teammates.

For example, a friend may perform poorly, but the player may avoid saying so because it could affect their friendship. A player may also join a team of strangers and feel that some teammates perform poorly, but feel uncomfortable expressing this directly.

Their solution allows players to provide anonymous feedback, reducing awkwardness and interpersonal conflict.

### 6. Addressing Unequal Power Within the Group

Some players may remain in a dominant position and always decide which game to play, which mode to choose, whom to invite, and how long the group will play.

To prevent decision-making power from remaining concentrated in the same people, they proposed randomly selecting a decision-maker.

The randomly selected player decides the arrangements for that session, giving everyone a fair opportunity to make decisions.

### 7. Potential Problems with Inviting Players

Invited players may already have conflicts with each other and may not want to play together.

Having the right to invite others does not mean that they must continue playing for the entire session.

Invited players could first try playing one or two rounds. If they do not want to continue, they should be allowed to leave afterward.

The main goal is to give everyone the opportunity to invite others and make choices, while also allowing them the freedom to leave.
