# Icebreaker UNO Interaction Test Feedback

## Participant 1

### 1. At what points, if any, were you unsure what to do next?

It was easy to follow after the initial explanation, although I sometimes wasn’t sure whether to look at my phone or the shared display.

### 2. What helped or stopped you from starting a conversation with another participant?

UNO gave us something to talk about immediately, and the questions helped us learn about each other beyond the game.

### 3. When did you feel most comfortable or uncomfortable interacting with others, and why?

I became more comfortable as we played, but having the tutor nearby made me more careful about what I said.

### 4. How did the prototype affect your interaction with other people? Can you describe a specific moment?

The colours changing based on our similarity were quite cool and gave us something extra to talk about. They were a little useful for noticing shared interests, although checking different screens sometimes distracted me from the conversation.

### 5. What would you change to make this work better with unfamiliar classmates in a real tutorial, and why?

I would combine the main game information into one display and have the tutor step back after explaining the activity.

## Participant 2

### 1. At what points, if any, were you unsure what to do next?

Most of it was straightforward. Having the game information together on one display would make it easier to follow without checking multiple places.

### 2. What helped or stopped you from starting a conversation with another participant?

The questions were useful, especially at the beginning. Sometimes we became focused on our cards and the conversation stopped.

### 3. When did you feel most comfortable or uncomfortable interacting with others, and why?

Playing together felt more relaxed than introducing ourselves formally. I also liked being able to skip questions without stopping the game.

### 4. How did the prototype affect your interaction with other people? Can you describe a specific moment?

The prompts gave us topics we probably would not have brought up ourselves. Between prompts, though, there were a few quiet moments when nobody knew what to say.

### 5. What would you change to make this work better with unfamiliar classmates in a real tutorial, and why?

I would simplify the displays and add an optional conversation prompt when the table has been quiet for a while. It should be easy to dismiss if everyone is just concentrating.

## Participant 3

### 1. At what points, if any, were you unsure what to do next?

Once we started playing, I understood the main actions. Being able to answer a question later helped me keep up with the game.

### 2. What helped or stopped you from starting a conversation with another participant?

The game and questions made it easier to talk, but sometimes our answers were short and the conversation did not continue. A follow-up prompt could help.

### 3. When did you feel most comfortable or uncomfortable interacting with others, and why?

I enjoyed the activity, but the tutor’s presence made it feel slightly like we were being assessed. I think we would have talked more openly among ourselves.

### 4. How did the prototype affect your interaction with other people? Can you describe a specific moment?

The questions helped us find shared interests. I liked having that support, although I would not want extra prompts interrupting a conversation that was already going well. The changing colours were cool as well and somewhat useful for spotting similarities.

### 5. What would you change to make this work better with unfamiliar classmates in a real tutorial, and why?

The tutor could introduce the activity and then give us some space. An optional prompt after a longer silence could also help us restart the conversation.
