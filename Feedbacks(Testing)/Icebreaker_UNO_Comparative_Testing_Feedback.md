# Icebreaker UNO Comparative Testing Feedback


## Participant 1

### 1. How did your experience differ between the questions-only round and the UNO-with-questions round?

The questions only round felt a bit like an interview. With UNO, we had something to do together, so it felt less like we were taking turns answering questions about ourselves.

### 2. In which round, if either, did you feel more comfortable talking to the other participants? What made you feel that way?

I felt more comfortable during UNO. Having cards to look at made the pauses less awkward because I didn’t feel like I had to keep making eye contact with people I had just met.

### 3. How did playing UNO affect your ability to listen and respond to other people’s answers?

Sometimes I was thinking about my next card and missed part of someone’s answer. But overall, feeling more relaxed made it easier for me to respond.

### 4. In which round did conversations develop beyond the provided questions? Can you describe an example?

It happened more during UNO. A question about hobbies lead into talking about games we liked, and the game itself gave us things to joke about.

### 5. How did your willingness to share something about yourself differ between the two rounds? Did either round make you feel pressured to answer?

I was more willing to share during UNO because it felt casual. In the questions only round, I felt more pressure to give an interesting answer when everyone was waiting for me.

### 6. Which format would you prefer for meeting unfamiliar classmates, and what would you change to improve it?

I would choose UNO with questions. I would ask the questions during short pauses so we could listen properly without also trying to play our cards.

## Participant 2

### 1. How did your experience differ between the questions-only round and the UNO-with-questions round?

Questions only helped me learn about the others more quickly, but it felt formal at first. UNO took some attention away from the awkwardness of meeting strangers.

### 2. In which round, if either, did you feel more comfortable talking to the other participants? What made you feel that way?

I preferred talking while playing UNO. Even when nobody was speaking, we were still doing something together, so the silence didn’t feel like a problem.

### 3. How did playing UNO affect your ability to listen and respond to other people’s answers?

UNO sometimes split my attention. If it was my turn, I focused on the cards instead of thinking of a follow-up question. Listening was easier in the questions-only round.

### 4. In which round did conversations develop beyond the provided questions? Can you describe an example?

Both formats could lead to longer conversations. Questions only made it easier to follow up on an answer, while UNO gave us more spontaneous comments and jokes.

### 5. How did your willingness to share something about yourself differ between the two rounds? Did either round make you feel pressured to answer?

I felt comfortable sharing small things in both rounds, but UNO made it easier to start. I would still want to be able to skip a personal question without having to explain why.

### 6. Which format would you prefer for meeting unfamiliar classmates, and what would you change to improve it?

I would start with UNO and then have a short conversation afterwards. Once we had relaxed a little, I think answering questions without playing would feel less awkward.

## Participant 3

### 1. How did your experience differ between the questions-only round and the UNO-with-questions round?

UNO was more fun and relaxed, but questions only made it easier to concentrate on getting to know people. I liked different parts of each format.

### 2. In which round, if either, did you feel more comfortable talking to the other participants? What made you feel that way?

UNO helped me feel comfortable at the beginning. However, once we had started talking, I liked the attention people gave each other during questions only.

### 3. How did playing UNO affect your ability to listen and respond to other people’s answers?

I found it easier to listen without the cards. During UNO, I sometimes wanted to ask about someone’s answer, but the next turn started and the moment passed.

### 4. In which round did conversations develop beyond the provided questions? Can you describe an example?

Questions only gave me more space to ask follow-up questions, especially when someone mentioned an interest we shared. During UNO, more of our extra conversation was about what was happening in the game.

### 5. How did your willingness to share something about yourself differ between the two rounds? Did either round make you feel pressured to answer?

Questions only initially made me feel a little put on the spot, but being listened to helped. UNO reduced that initial pressure, although I tended to give shorter answers while playing.

### 6. Which format would you prefer for meeting unfamiliar classmates, and what would you change to improve it?

I would use UNO as a short warm-up, followed by questions. I would also let people continue an interesting conversation instead of moving straight to the next question or turn.
