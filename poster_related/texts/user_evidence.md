## User Evidence

### What worked

Across multiple rounds of user testing, participants reported that **UNO helped reduce the awkwardness of meeting unfamiliar students**. Compared with a questions-only activity, playing UNO created a more relaxed environment where participants could interact naturally without the pressure of formal introductions. The game provided a shared activity that made silences less uncomfortable and encouraged spontaneous conversations.

Optional conversation questions helped participants discover common interests and learn more about one another beyond the game. Participants particularly valued being able to skip questions, allowing them to control how much personal information they shared. Changing colour indicators also encouraged curiosity by highlighting similarities between players, providing additional opportunities to start conversations.

> “Playing gave us something to talk about without having to introduce ourselves formally.”
>
> — Participant 1, Feature Testing

### What needed attention

Although participants enjoyed the activity, testing revealed several challenges. **Balancing gameplay with meaningful conversation was a recurring issue**, as participants sometimes focused on their cards instead of listening to others. Interesting conversations could also be interrupted when the next turn or rule change occurred.

The use of multiple screens occasionally divided participants' attention between their phones, the shared display and the people around them. Some participants found the colour indicators and similarity percentages confusing, making it difficult to interpret what the feedback represented.

Additionally, participants reported feeling more self-conscious when tutors were observing, suggesting that the activity would benefit from a less evaluative environment. Feedback highlighted the importance of **simpler interactions, clearer visual feedback and greater freedom to communicate naturally**.

> “I sometimes wasn’t sure whether to look at my phone or the shared display.”
>
> — Participant 1, Interaction Testing

### Refinements in the final prototype

Responding to these findings, we refined the prototype to **prioritise face-to-face interaction while reducing digital distractions**. Core game information was consolidated onto one shared screen, with optional phone interactions allowing participants to answer or skip questions and receive similarity notifications.

Clearer instructions and explanations were introduced to help participants understand changing colours and similarity results. The **Pause feature** allows players to stop both gameplay and the timer, giving conversations time to develop without being interrupted by the next turn.

We also retained optional questions to preserve participants' autonomy and reduce pressure to disclose personal information. Final testing reinforced the value of these choices, while identifying further opportunities to improve the experience, including clearer notifications and more accessible explanations of similarity feedback.

> “I would make more use of the pause button when an interesting conversation starts.”
>
> — Participant 3, Final Prototype Testing
