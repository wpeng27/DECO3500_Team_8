Social — Collaborative

1. Shared Objects & Space

Players interact through physical UNO cards and a shared screen before being expected to talk directly about themselves.

2. Coordination

Turn-taking, shared rules and pause controls help players coordinate their actions without needing to negotiate the entire process themselves.

3. Optional Conversation

Players can draw a question, choose an answer or skip it on the shared screen or their own phone. Technology invites conversation without forcing personal disclosure.

4. Awareness & Shared Ground

Shared colour feedback and match alerts on players' phones help them notice similar answers and possible common ground, without claiming to measure personality or team compatibility.

5. Distributed Control

There is no fixed host. Players have equal control over pausing, continuing and choosing whether to participate.

Mobile — Contextual

1. Context-Specific

After a tutor announces self-formed groups, unfamiliar students are physically present but may not know whom or how to approach.

2. Location-Specific

The experience is designed around:

- the same tutorial room;
- the same table;
- face-to-face players;
- physical UNO cards;
- a shared screen visible to everyone; and
- optional phone screens connected over the same Wi-Fi.

3. Interaction-Context Responsive

The interface responds to the current game state, including occupied seats, active rules, questions, answers and pauses.

4. Transient

The experience supports a short tutorial-session interaction rather than creating permanent profiles or long-term assessments. The app only keeps a simple record of who took part, stored on the shared-screen computer, and phone connections expire after the session.

5. Public–Private Balance

Game rules and shared progress remain public on the shared screen, while each player's phone shows their own colour and match alerts, and players control whether they answer or skip a question.
