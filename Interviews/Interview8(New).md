# Interview Transcript

**Interview Topic:** Experiences of Forming Course Groups with Unfamiliar Classmates  
**Participant:** International university student, recently arrived in Australia  
**Interview Format:** Semi-structured interview  
**Transcript Type:** Clean Verbatim Transcript  

---

## 1. Group Formation Context

**Interviewer:** Think about the most recent time you had to form a course group with classmates you did not know well. What course was it? Were the groups self-formed or assigned?

**Participant:** The most recent time was in one of my courses this semester. We had a group assignment, and I think there were supposed to be around four people in each group.

The groups were self-formed. I actually find that more stressful than assigned groups because, if the tutor assigns the groups, at least I don’t have to approach people myself.

I’m quite new to Australia, so at that time I didn’t really know many people in the class. I recognised some students because I had seen them before, but I hadn’t really talked to them.

---

## 2. Group Formation Process

**Interviewer:** Could you walk me through what happened, from when the group task was announced to when your group was finally formed?

**Participant:** When the tutor first announced that we needed to form our own groups, I immediately started worrying about it.

I looked around the room, and some people started talking to their friends straight away. It felt like a lot of people already knew who they wanted to work with.

I didn’t approach anyone immediately. I was mostly watching to see who was still available.

I think I was worried that, if I asked someone, they might say their group was already full. That would be a bit embarrassing, especially because I would still have to see them in class afterwards.

So I waited for a while.

Eventually, another student asked me whether I already had a group. I said no, and then we started talking. After that, we found two other students who also didn’t have groups yet.

So, to be honest, I don’t think we really selected each other based on anything specific. It was more like we were all still looking for a group at the same time.

Once the group was finally formed, I felt very relieved. Before that, I was worried that everyone would find a group and I would be left by myself.

---

## 3. Decision-Making

**Interviewer:** How did you decide whom to approach, which group to join, or whom to accept as a group member? What information did you use to make that decision?

**Participant:** I think the first thing I notice is whether someone looks approachable.

If someone is sitting alone, I feel more comfortable talking to them. If someone is already talking with several friends, I normally assume they already have a group, so I probably won’t approach them.

I also notice things from class, like whether someone attends regularly, pays attention, or participates in discussions.

But that information is quite limited.

I don’t really know whether they are reliable, whether they submit things on time, or what they are like when actually working in a group.

For me, communication is also important because English isn’t my first language. If someone seems patient and friendly when speaking with international students, I feel much more comfortable working with them.

---

## 4. Hesitation and Concerns

**Interviewer:** Was there a moment when you hesitated to contact someone or join a group? If so, what were you concerned about? What did you eventually do?

**Participant:** Yes, definitely.

There was one student in the class who seemed very organised. They answered questions quite often, and I thought they would probably be a good person to work with.

I considered approaching them, but I didn’t.

I think there were several things going through my mind. I was worried they might already have a group, or that they might not want me in their group.

I was also a little worried about my English. Sometimes I worry that people might think working with an international student will make the assignment more difficult.

Even if nobody has actually said that to me, it is still something I think about.

So, in the end, I didn’t approach that person. I just waited until someone else spoke to me.

I know waiting probably makes the situation more difficult, but when I feel anxious, it feels safer.

---

## 5. Information Needs

**Interviewer:** What would help you judge whether someone might be a reliable or compatible group member? Was there anything you wanted to know but could not notice?

**Participant:** I would definitely like to know more about how they normally work in groups.

For example, whether they reply to messages, whether they finish their part on time, and whether they usually start assignments early or leave things until close to the deadline.

I think availability would also be useful. Sometimes group members have completely different schedules, and then it becomes very difficult to organise meetings.

It would also be useful to know people’s skills.

For example, someone might be good at research, writing, presentations, design, or data analysis. If you knew that beforehand, it might be easier to create a balanced group.

Personality matters too, but I don’t mean that everyone needs to be very outgoing. I think I would mainly want someone who communicates clearly and doesn’t suddenly disappear for several days.

The problem is that, when you form a group, normally you cannot see any of this information.

You might only know their name and recognise their face from class.

So the decision can feel quite random.

---

## 6. Additional Experiences

**Interviewer:** Is there anything important about forming groups with unfamiliar classmates that I have not asked about?

**Participant:** I think one thing that is difficult is the feeling of being excluded, even if nobody is intentionally excluding you.

When the tutor says, “Form your own groups,” people who already know each other immediately start talking.

If you are new, you are just sitting there and trying to work out who is still available.

It can make you feel like everyone else already belongs somewhere and you don’t.

There is also pressure because you feel like you need to find a group quickly.

The longer you wait, the more you start thinking that all the groups will become full.

Because of that, sometimes you just join whoever is available. You don’t really have enough time or information to think about whether you would actually work well together.

I think being an international student makes that harder because, at the same time, you are adjusting to a new country, speaking another language, learning how university works here, and trying to meet new people.

---

## 7. Friends Afterwards

**Interviewer:** Have you ever met someone through the group-forming process but not ended up being teammates, yet still stayed in contact or became friends?

**Participant:** Yes, that happened once.

I spoke to another student while people were forming groups, but their group became full, so we didn’t end up working together.

After that, though, we still recognised each other in class.

Because we had already spoken once, it was much easier to say hello the next time.

Later, we exchanged Instagram accounts and sometimes talked before class.

We are not very close friends, but I think we became more comfortable with each other.

Without that first conversation during the group-forming process, I probably wouldn’t have approached them.

---

## 8. Course Friends

**Interviewer:** Have you ever wanted to get to know someone from your course but felt unsure about approaching them because you had no reason to talk to them?

**Participant:** Yes. Quite often, actually.

Sometimes there are people in my class who seem friendly or interesting, and I think I would like to talk to them, but I don’t really know how to start.

If there is no assignment, activity, or specific reason to speak to them, approaching them suddenly feels a little strange to me.

I start thinking, “What am I supposed to say?” or, “Why would they want to talk to me?”

Sometimes I can sit near the same person for several weeks. We recognise each other, but we still never properly talk.

I think having even a small reason to interact would make it much easier.

For me, starting the first conversation is the hardest part. Once I have already spoken to someone, talking to them again feels much easier.

---

## 9. Interaction with Others

**Interviewer:** After a group is formed, do you usually continue interacting with classmates who aren't in your group? Why or why not?

**Participant:** Usually not very much.

After the group is formed, most of my communication is with my own group members because we have the assignment as a reason to keep talking.

With other classmates, I might smile, say hello, or have a short conversation, but I normally wouldn’t contact them outside class.

I think part of it is because I’m shy, but it is also because I don’t feel like I have a reason to contact them.

If I have already spoken to someone before, then it is different. I feel much more comfortable speaking to them again.

So I don’t think the problem is that I don’t want to meet other students. I actually would like to know more people from my course.

For me, the difficult part is finding a natural reason to start that first interaction.
