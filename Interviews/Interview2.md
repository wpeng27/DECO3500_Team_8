1. Group Formation Context
Think about the most recent time you had to form a course group with classmates you did not know well. What course was it? Were the groups self-formed or assigned?

It was for the course Introduction to Software Innovation. The groups were assigned by the teacher. The teacher directly announced who was in each group, so we didn’t choose or find our own group members.


2. Group Formation Process
Could you walk me through what happened, from when the group task was announced to when your group was finally formed?

The teacher first explained that we would complete the course project in groups. After that, the teacher announced the group list, including the names of the students in each group. My name is in Group 8. So I'm going to look for the table number 8.


3.Decision-Making
How did you decide whom to approach, which group to join, or whom to accept as a group member? What information did you use to make that decision?

I did not decide whom to approach or which group to join because the groups were assigned by the teacher. Before working together, I did not know much about my group members, such as their skills, working styles or schedules. We only started learning about each other after the group had already been formed.


4. Hesitation and Concerns
Was there a moment when you hesitated to contact someone or join a group? If so, what were you concerned about? What did you eventually do?

I did not hesitate about joining the group because I did not need to contact people and ask to join them. However, I was a little concerned because I did not know the other group members well. I was unsure whether everyone would communicate actively, complete their work on time and contribute equally. I decided to introduce myself and communicate with them first.


5. Information Needs
What information would help you judge whether someone might be a reliable or compatible group member? Was there anything you wanted to know but could not find out?

It would be helpful to know each person’s skills, availability, preferred role and working style. For example, I would want to know whether they prefer coding, design, research or presentation work. I would also want to know whether they usually complete tasks on time and communicate regularly. I could not know these things before the teacher assigned the groups, so we had to learn about each other while completing the project.


6. Additional Experiences
Is there anything important about forming groups with unfamiliar classmates that I have not asked about?

I think assigned groups can be helpful because they give students a chance to work with unfamiliar classmates. However, it may take some time for group members to understand each other’s strengths and working habits. Having a short introduction or team discussion at the beginning could make the collaboration easier.

7. Friends Afterwards
Have you ever met someone through the group-forming process but not ended up being teammates, yet still stayed in contact or became friends？

Yes. I have met classmates through group-forming activities who did not end up in my group. We still stayed in contact because we were taking the same course and sometimes discussed assignments or helped each other with course content.


9. Course Friends
Have you ever wanted to get to know someone from your course but felt unsure about approaching them because you had no reason to talk to them?

Yes. Sometimes I wanted to get to know someone from my course, but I felt unsure about approaching them because we had never worked together and I did not have a clear reason to start a conversation. I was worried that suddenly talking to them might feel awkward.


11. Interaction with others
After a group is formed, do you usually continue interacting with classmates who aren't in your group? Why or why not?

After a group is formed, I usually interact more with my own group members because we need to communicate regularly about the project. I may still talk to classmates outside my group during class or discuss course content with them, but usually not as often because we do not have a shared task that requires regular communication.


Observation

Assigned grouping can reduce hesitation about approaching unfamiliar classmates.

Students may still feel concerned about communication, equal contribution, and completing work on time.

Skills, availability, preferred roles, and working styles are important information for understanding group compatibility.

Students usually learn about their teammates’ strengths and habits only after they begin working together.

Assigned groups can provide opportunities to collaborate with unfamiliar classmates.

A short introduction or team discussion at the beginning could help members understand each other and collaborate more easily.
