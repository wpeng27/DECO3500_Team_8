## Interview Transcript

**Q1:** Think about the most recent time you had to form a course group with classmates you did not know well. What course was it? Were the groups self-formed or assigned?

**A:** I think it was last semester, for one of my design courses. We had to form the group by ourselves, I think four people in one group. I knew some people in the class like I recognise their faces, but I didn't really know them personally. I came with one friend so at least I know I probably gonna group with him, but for the other two people we didn't really have a plan.


**Q2:** Could you walk me through what happened, from when the group task was announced to when your group was finally formed?

**A:** Yeah, so the tutor told us about the project and then basically said you can start finding your group. Everyone just started talking with the people around them. Me and my friend were sitting together, and there was another guy next to us who I talked with maybe once before, so we asked him if he already have a group. He said no, so then there was three of us.

Then we still need one more person. There were some people walking around and talking to different tables, but it's kind of hard to know who already have a group. Eventually this girl came to our table because she also didn't have one, and she asked us if we need another person. So yeah, that's basically how the group happened. It wasn't really like we carefully choose everyone, it was more just who was there at that time.


**Q3:** How did you decide whom to approach, which group to join, or whom to accept as a group member? What information did you use to make that decision?

**A:** Mostly I think it's just who I already talked to before, even just a little bit. Like if I recognise someone from tutorial and I remember they were quite active or friendly, I feel more easy to talk with them. If it's completely stranger, I probably wouldn't just walk across the room and ask them unless I really need to.

I also look at who they are sitting with. If there's already four people talking together, I just assume they already have a group, even maybe they don't. But if someone is sitting by themselves or looking around, then I feel maybe they are also looking for people.


**Q4:** Was there a moment when you hesitated to contact someone or join a group? If so, what were you concerned about? What did you eventually do?

**A:** Yeah definitely. There was one table I thought about asking because I knew one person there, but they were all talking already and it looked like they know each other quite well. I don't know if they already formed the group or just friends talking, so I didn't want to interrupt them and then they say like, sorry we're full. It's a bit awkward.

So I just stayed with my friend and waited for a while. Actually that's probably why the last person joined us instead of us finding her, because we didn't really want to keep asking different people.


**Q5:** What would help you judge whether someone might be a reliable or compatible group member? Was there anything you wanted to know but could not notice?

**A:** Maybe how they normally work in group projects. Like do they prefer finish things early or do everything near deadline, because that can be quite different. Also maybe what part they enjoy doing. But I don't think I need to know everything about them before making a group.

For me maybe the most useful thing at that moment is actually just knowing if they are still looking for a group. Because sometimes you see someone you might want to ask, but you don't know if they already promised their friends or already have a group. So you don't ask.


**Q6:** Is there anything important about forming groups with unfamiliar classmates that I have not asked about?

**A:** Maybe where you sit is actually quite important. Because usually you just talk with people close to you. If someone is on the other side of classroom, even if maybe we have similar interest, I probably never talk to them. Once everyone starts forming groups it happens quite fast as well, so if you wait too long, you feel like everyone else already got a group.


**Q7:** Have you ever met someone through the group-forming process but not ended up being teammates, yet still stayed in contact or became friends?

**A:** Yeah, actually I have. There was one time I was talking with two people when we were trying to make groups, but then we realised the group size doesn't work because they already had some other friends joining. So I didn't end up in their group.

But one of them I talked with for maybe ten minutes during that process, and after that when I saw him in the tutorial we would say hi or sometimes sit near each other. Later we also had another class together and we talked more. I wouldn't say we became like very close friends, but definitely I know him because of that group forming moment. If we didn't talk that day, probably we still just recognise each other's face but never actually speak.


**Q8:** Have you ever wanted to get to know someone from your course but felt unsure about approaching them because you had no reason to talk to them?

**A:** Yeah, quite a lot actually. Sometimes there's people I see every week and they seem interesting or they say something good in class, so I think maybe I want to know them. But it's weird to just suddenly go like, hi, I see you every week, let's be friends. There's no natural reason for that.

Group work sometimes gives you that reason, because you can ask like, “Do you have a group?” and then the conversation can start from there. Even if you don't become group members, at least now you talked once, so next time it's much easier to say something. I think the hardest part is probably just the first conversation.


**Q9:** After a group is formed, do you usually continue interacting with classmates who aren't in your group? Why or why not?

**A:** Not really, unless I already talked with them before. Once the groups are formed, everyone kind of stays with their own group because most class activities are about your project. So there's less reason to talk with other people.

Sometimes I still talk with people from other groups before class or ask what they are doing for their project, especially if I already know them a little bit. But for people I never talked with before the group was formed, probably I won't suddenly start after. I think after the groups are made, the class becomes a little bit separated, like you know your four people and other people are doing their own thing.


## Observations

- Physical proximity strongly influences who students interact with during group formation.
- Students use visible social cues to guess whether others are available or open to forming a group.
- Students may avoid approaching groups that already appear socially established, even when they are unsure whether the group is full.
- Knowing who is still looking for a group may be more immediately useful than having detailed information about potential teammates.
- Students often rely on existing familiarity, even minimal previous interaction, when deciding whom to approach.
- Group formation can create new social connections even between students who do not eventually become teammates.
- Having a clear reason to interact can make approaching unfamiliar classmates feel easier.
- The first interaction appears to be an important barrier when students want to connect with unfamiliar classmates.
- Once groups are formed, opportunities to interact with students outside the group may decrease.
- Classroom seating and the speed of the group-forming process can shape who students have an opportunity to meet.
