# Interview Transcript

**Participant profile:**  
A new international student who has recently started studying at UQ. They are not confident speaking English and feel especially anxious about random or teacher-assigned group work.

---

## Q1

**Think about the most recent time you had to form a course group with classmates you did not know well. What course was it, and were the groups self-formed or assigned?**

**A:**  
I think it was in one of my first courses at UQ. I was still very new here, so I didn't really know many people in the class. The teacher asked us to make groups, but I was not very sure if we could choose by ourselves or if the teacher would put us into groups.

At that time I was actually very nervous because my English is not very good. I was really afraid the teacher would randomly put me with people I don't know. If I can choose by myself, maybe I can find someone I already talked with before, so I feel a little safer.

---

## Q2

**Could you walk me through what happened, from when the group task was announced to when your group was finally formed?**

**A:**  
At first, the teacher explained that we would need to work in groups for the assignment. When I heard "group assignment", I already started to feel a little stressed because I was thinking, "Who am I going to work with?"

I looked around the classroom, but most people were already talking with their friends. I didn't really know how to go to another table and ask them if I could join. I was worried they already had enough people, or maybe they didn't want to work with me because my English is not good.

For a few minutes I just stayed in my seat and waited. Then I saw another international student that I had talked to once before, so I went to ask if they already had a group. They said they were also looking for people, so I joined them. After that, we found some other students and finally made the group.

I felt much better after the group was formed, but before that it was quite stressful.

---

## Q3

**How did you decide whom to approach, which group to join, or whom to accept as a group member? What information did you use?**

**A:**  
For me, I didn't really think about their skills at that time because I was still new and I didn't know who was good at programming, design, or other things.

The first thing I thought about was whether I felt comfortable talking with them. If I already talked with someone before, even just one or two times, I would prefer to join them because at least I know them a little bit.

I also looked for people who seemed friendly and patient. Because my English is not very good, sometimes I need more time to understand what people are saying. If someone talks very fast or looks impatient, I feel more afraid to work with them.

So for me, communication comfort was probably more important than skills when I first came to UQ.

---

## Q4

**What information would help you judge whether someone might be a reliable or compatible group member? Was there anything you wanted to know but could not find out?**

**A:**  
I think I would like to know their working style and expectations.

For example, I want to know if they want to get a very high grade or they are just trying to pass the course. I also want to know how often they want to meet, how quickly they usually reply to messages, and what kind of tasks they are comfortable doing.

For me, another important thing is communication. I want to know if they are okay working with someone whose English is not very strong. Sometimes I understand the task, but I cannot explain my idea very quickly in English. I worry people may think I don't understand or I'm not contributing.

But before joining a group, normally you cannot know these things. You only see someone's name or maybe you have seen them in class. So it's difficult to know if we will work well together.

---

## Q5

**Is there anything important about forming groups with unfamiliar classmates that I have not asked about?**

**A:**  
I think knowing the current group situation is very important.

Sometimes I don't know which students already have a group, which group still needs another person, or who is still looking for teammates. I feel embarrassed to ask people one by one because maybe they already have their friends.

It would be easier if there was some way to see, for example, "this group has three people and needs one more person", or "this student is still looking for a team".

I also think it would help if I could know a little bit about people before I contact them, like what skills they have, what kind of grade they want, and maybe what language they are comfortable using.

For a new student, that would make the process much less scary.

---

## Q6

**Was there a moment when you hesitated to contact someone or join a group? If so, what were you concerned about, and what did you eventually do?**

**A:**  
Yes, definitely.

There were some students who looked very confident and active in class, and actually I wanted to work with them because I thought I could learn a lot from them. But I didn't contact them.

The biggest reason was my English. I was afraid that if I joined their group, I couldn't follow the discussion. Maybe they would speak very quickly, and I would need them to repeat things many times. I didn't want to slow the group down.

I was also worried they might judge me because I cannot explain my ideas clearly in English.

So normally, even if I think someone is very capable, I will choose someone who makes me feel more comfortable instead. Sometimes I prefer working with other international students because I feel they may understand the language problem better.

Teacher-assigned or completely random groups make me more nervous because I cannot control who I will work with. If I could see some information about the other students before the group was created, I think I would feel more prepared.

---

# Observations

- New international students may experience significant anxiety during group formation.
- Limited English confidence can strongly influence who students feel comfortable approaching.
- Communication comfort may initially be more important than perceived technical ability.
- Random or teacher-assigned groups can create anxiety because students have little control over who they will work with.
- Students may avoid highly capable classmates if they fear slowing them down or being judged.
- Familiarity, even from very limited previous interaction, can make a teammate feel significantly safer.
- Students want to know whether classmates are already in a group or still looking for teammates.
- Information about communication preferences, working style, availability, skills, and grade expectations could reduce uncertainty.
- International students may prefer teammates who seem patient or who may understand language barriers.
- Fear of rejection can discourage students from approaching unfamiliar groups.
- Group formation is not only a skills-matching problem; confidence, language, psychological safety, and perceived acceptance strongly shape decisions.
- Providing information before group formation may help students feel more prepared for teacher-assigned or random groups.
