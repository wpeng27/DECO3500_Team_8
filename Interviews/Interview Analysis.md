## Labels and Highlighted Statements

Short label applied to a recurring idea, with the strongest supporting quote.

### Label: **Random/circumstantial joining**
- P1: *"I just random pick a seat, find a table which have the people I am similar with them, so I just sit in, that's all."*
- P6: *"It wasn't really like we carefully choose everyone, it was more just who was there at that time."*
- P7: *"Everyone just started talking with the people around them."*

### Label: **Physical proximity drives approach**
- P6: *"Maybe where you sit is actually quite important... if someone is on the other side of classroom... I probably never talk to them."*
- P7: *"I initially talked to a couple of people I had spoken to in previous classes."*

### Label: **Prior micro-familiarity as safety net**
- P1 (later semesters): *"I do know some of the people who have the skills"*
- P3: *"a lot of it was just recognition... I've seen this person before, they seem normal."*
- P6: *"Mostly I think it's just who I already talked to before, even just a little bit."*

### Label: **Engagement/participation as a proxy signal**
- P3: *"whether people asked questions in the tutorial, because that at least tells you they're engaged."*
- P7: *"whether they participated in tutorials, whether they seemed engaged in class."*

### Label: **Uncertainty about who's "still available"**
- P4: *"I don't know which students already have a group, which group still needs another person."*
- P6: *"the most useful thing at that moment is actually just knowing if they are still looking for a group."*
- P3: implicit - worry about "already had their numbers sorted."

### Label: **Fear of rejection / looking desperate**
- P3: *"I was worried it'd come across as a bit desperate, like please let me in."*
- P6: *"I didn't want to interrupt them and then they say like, sorry we're full."*
- P7: *"whether it would be awkward if they said no."*

### Label: **Reliability > skill**
- P3: *"the biggest thing I want to know is just will this person actually do the work?"*
- P7: *"I'd rather have someone who's reliable and communicates than someone who's really skilled but disappears for two weeks."*
- P2: values "complete their work on time and contribute equally."

### Label: **Desire for a "trust/reliability signal"**
- P3: *"a reliability rating from past group work... did their previous groupmates think they pulled their weight."*
- P5: *"I think better to check the GPA at the worst stage."*
- P7: wants to know deadline habits and workload expectations up front.

### Label: **Language/confidence as a filtering criterion (international students)**
- P1: avoided a highly capable teammate because *"I feel like I'm kind of delay everything... unconfident and uncomfortable."*
- P4: *"communication comfort was probably more important than skills."*
- P5: avoids fluent English speakers due to *"invisible sense of oppression."*

### Label: **Self-screening away from high performers**
- P1: *"if I have the second chance, definitely I was not going to be in the same team with her, even though I know she is really good at everything."*
- P4: *"even if I think someone is very capable, I will choose someone who makes me feel more comfortable instead."*
- P5: avoids fluent speakers regardless of competence.

### Label: **Domestic/international self-segregation**
- P3: *"international students and local students sometimes end up grouping separately... not on purpose."*
- P4/P5: gravitate toward other international students for comfort.

### Label: **Procrastination / passive waiting**
- P3: *"First week I didn't really do anything, I just kinda watched who was talking to who."*
- P5: delayed until the professor was about to allocate leftover students.
- P4: *"For a few minutes I just stayed in my seat and waited."*

### Label: **Time pressure / narrow window**
- P6: *"Once everyone starts forming groups it happens quite fast... if you wait too long, you feel like everyone else already got a group."*
- P7: *"there's a limited window where you're supposed to find a group."*

### Label: **Assigned groups reduce approach-anxiety but not outcome-anxiety**
- P2: didn't hesitate to join (no approach needed) but *"was a little concerned because I did not know the other group members well... unsure whether everyone would communicate actively."*

### Label: **Relationships born from group-forming, even without becoming teammates**
- P6: *"one of them I talked with for maybe ten minutes during that process... I know him because of that group forming moment."*
- P7: *"we still ended up talking occasionally afterwards."*
- P5 is the outlier: *"No"* to every relationship-persistence question - purely transactional.

### Label: **Group-forming as a socially license-giving "reason to talk"**
- P6: *"Group work sometimes gives you that reason... next time it's much easier to say something."*
- P7: *"Group projects are probably one of the easiest ways to get around that because there's already a natural reason to start a conversation."*

### Label: **Post-formation "bubble" effect**
- P2: *"we do not have a shared task that requires regular communication"* (with non-groupmates).
- P6: *"the class becomes a little bit separated."*
- P7: *"the group itself can become a bit of a bubble."*

### Label: **Desire for tools to see availability/compatibility info before committing**
- P2: wants skills, availability, preferred role, working style visible.
- P4: wants a way to see group status and skill/language info before contacting.
- P7: *"if there was an easy way to stay connected with other classmates... I'd probably use it."*

### Label: **Individual variation in need for connection**
- P5: *"I don't really mind, due to I have the confidence that I can handle the workload on my own."*
- Contrasts sharply with P1/P4's high anxiety - signals the population is not homogeneous.

## From Labels to Themes

### Theme A - Group formation is largely opportunistic, not deliberate
Labels: Random/circumstantial joining, Physical proximity, Prior micro-familiarity, Procrastination, Time pressure.
Across nearly every self-formed scenario (P1, P3, P4, P5, P6, P7), the actual mechanism was proximity and timing rather than careful matching. Even participants who *say* they evaluate people end up describing outcomes driven by "who happened to be near me" or "who was left over when time ran out."

### Theme B - Visible/surface cues substitute for real information
Labels: Engagement as proxy signal, Desire for reliability signal, Reliability > skill.
Students consistently lack access to the information they actually want (reliability, work habits, GPA, past group performance) and fall back on weak proxies - talkativeness, eye contact, tutorial participation - because that's all that's observable in a classroom setting.

### Theme C - Uncertainty about "market status" (who's available) creates friction
Labels: Uncertainty about availability, Fear of rejection, Time pressure.
A recurring, very concrete pain point (P3, P4, P6, P7) is not "who is a good teammate" but simply "who still needs a group?" Because this isn't visible, students avoid approaching tables that might already be full, which produces missed connections and delays.

### Theme D - Language and confidence gaps drive a distinct "status anxiety," not generic shyness
Labels: Language as filtering criterion, Self-screening away from high performers, Domestic/international segregation.
This is the sharpest and most novel theme (especially P1, P4, P5). International or less-confident students actively avoid highly competent or fluent peers - not because they dislike them, but because working with them triggers feelings of inadequacy ("invisible sense of oppression," "I feel like I'm kind of delay everything"). This is a competence-comparison anxiety layered on top of ordinary stranger anxiety, and it actively works against optimal skill-based matching.

### Theme E - Group formation has a social afterlife independent of the group itself
Labels: Relationships from unsuccessful matches, Group-forming as a reason to talk, Post-formation bubble.
For most participants (P2, P6, P7), the group-forming *process itself* - not the group - is what creates a legitimate, low-awkwardness "reason" to start talking to a stranger. Some of these contacts persist as loose acquaintances even when the pairing doesn't happen. But P5's uniformly negative answers show this social spillover is not universal - some students treat the whole process as strictly transactional.

### Theme F - Structural condition (assigned vs. self-formed) changes *which* anxiety shows up, not whether anxiety exists
Labels: Assigned groups reduce approach-anxiety, individual variation.
P2's assigned-group experience removes the "will they accept me" fear entirely, but replaces it with a different worry: "will these strangers actually pull their weight?" So structure changes the *type* of uncertainty, not the *presence* of uncertainty.
